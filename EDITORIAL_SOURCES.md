# Editorial Sources Policy

End of the World Atlas uses a source hierarchy that favors documentary, cartographic and scientific materials over promotional or commercial sources. This policy applies to place dossiers, route dossiers, lighthouse references, maritime context and Antarctic threshold geography.

## Priority 1: Chilean Primary Sources

When documenting southern Chile, Patagonia, Tierra del Fuego, Puerto Williams, Cape Horn, Diego Ramírez Islands, the Strait of Magellan, Antarctic gateways and Antarctic logistics, Chilean primary sources should be consulted first whenever available.

Preferred sources include Chilean government agencies, scientific institutions, cartographic authorities and maritime authorities, including:

- Armada de Chile
- DIRECTEMAR
- INACH
- Instituto Geográfico Militar
- Gobierno Regional de Magallanes
- Municipalidad de Cabo de Hornos
- Ministerio de Obras Públicas
- Universidad de Magallanes

## Priority 2: Research and Scientific Data

Peer reviewed research, academic publications and scientific datasets should be used to clarify geography, climate, marine conditions, infrastructure, history and ecological context. These sources are especially important when primary institutional material is incomplete or requires technical interpretation.

## Priority 3: International References

International reference organizations, international media, travel publications and tourism publications may be used only as supporting context. They should not override Chilean primary sources when describing Chilean territory, maritime systems, public infrastructure, local institutions or Antarctic gateway logistics.

## Editorial Principle

The atlas should present southern geography in sober, verifiable language. Source selection must support a documentary and cartographic reading of place, route, coastline, channel, port, lighthouse and threshold geography, without relying on commercial tourism framing.
